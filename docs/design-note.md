# Design note: lockfamily.dev's identity

Issue #17. The site looked like any AI product page: navy, violet focus,
one neon per product, soft glows, a system sans, a centred stack of slogan
sections. Nothing in it said what the Lock Family is: **the agent asks, a
Lock holds the keys, a human signs a masked ticket outside the agent's
harness.** The identity starts from that sentence.

## Three directions

Quick mock-ups of the three, side by side: [mockups/directions.svg](mockups/directions.svg).

### A. Industrial: padlocks, key cuts, stamped metal (rejected)

Brushed steel, engraved labels, key-cut edges, a stencil face. It reads as
"security" at once, which is also its problem: it is the stock imagery of
every vault and VPN product, it carries the idea of a lock but not of the
*human decision*, and its textures cost bytes or turn into gradients. What
survives: engraved, single-weight line drawings for the logos, and the
padlock shackle as the family's common shape.

### B. Terminal-native, taken seriously (rejected as the whole identity)

The page as a console: monospace everywhere, real screens, the hub as a
process list. It is honest, since `lox run` is the product's centre, but a
whole page of mono is hard to read for the long explanations this site
needs, and green-on-black is the very "AI tool" cliché the issue names.
What survives: every terminal on the page is a real one (the quick starts,
the lox run screen in the animated flow), dark in both themes, in lox run's
own colours.

### C. The signed ticket (chosen)

A printed document: paper and ink, ruled ledger lines, a ticket with a
perforated stub, a stamp, a signature. It is the one direction that carries
the project's specific idea: a request becomes a *ticket*; the hub only
ever holds the masked ticket; a human (or a rule) *signs* the decision; the
audit log is a *ledger*. It also explains itself: a visitor sees one real
ticket in the hero and reads its life, line by line, in the first section.

- **Type**: Zilla Slab (display: the slab of forms and typewritten
  labels), Source Serif 4 (text: a document, not a dashboard), IBM Plex
  Mono (the ticket's fields, commands, product names). All OFL, self-hosted.
- **Colour**: paper and ink, plus one ink per meaning, never per product:
  signature blue for a human's decision and links, stamp red for a denial,
  teal for masked values, amber for "asked", green for "auto". The dark
  theme is carbon paper, warm, designed on its own.
- **Layout**: the hero is a statement next to a real ticket; the Locks are a
  ledger, not a card grid; section 01 is one write's audit ledger, then the
  animated flow; section 03 puts the hub in the centre, Locks on one side,
  the facades you answer from on the other. Sections are numbered clauses
  opened by a rule.
- **Copy**: concrete statements of what happens ("Queues the ticket for lox
  run. The agent has no route to it") instead of slogans ("Your AI does the
  work. You hold the key.", "Know the limits" became "Known limits").
- **Logos**: one engraved line style, generated by `tools/logo_svg.py`.
  The family mark is a padlock whose body is a ticket stub.

## Constraints kept

Static HTML/CSS/JS with no build step; no third-party request (fonts in
`assets/fonts/`); no tracking; WCAG AA contrast for every text token in both
themes (checked by `tests/test_site.py`, and in the browser by
`tests/site_e2e.py`); no horizontal scroll at 360px; `install.sh` and its URL
unchanged. The animated flow (`tools/flow_svg.py`) uses the same palette;
as an `<img>` on GitHub it cannot load web fonts, so it keeps a system font
stack.

The tokens, for the per-tool sites: [identity.md](identity.md).
